Match the method to the conditions.

Australian Government guidance distinguishes dry-climate evaporative cooling from cooling in humid conditions. Portable refrigerated air conditioners need an exhaust path outdoors. A fan cools people rather than lowering the room temperature.

01

Fans & climate-fit cooling

For a bedroom, rental or home office. Decide whether you need personal cooling or a reduction in room temperature, then check the conditions where you actually live.

Check before buying

  • A portable air conditioner and an evaporative cooler are different appliances; inspect the cooling method before comparing prices.
  • Compare noise information, controls and the space required around the device.
  • Use your own electricity tariff in AUD and model-specific input power to estimate costs; retailer claims are not your bill.
  • Before choosing portable air conditioning: check the exit for hot air

02

Window kits & flyscreen fit

For sliding, awning and casement windows. A cooling appliance that looks right on paper still needs an installation that works with the opening, screen and hose connection.

Check before buying

  • Identify how the sash opens; measure the opening, not just the outside frame.
  • Photograph the flyscreen, handles and hinges so you can compare them with kit drawings.
  • Check the actual hose connector and supported kit dimensions. A similar diameter alone does not establish compatibility.

Shade & everyday airflow

For sun-exposed rooms and warmer evenings. Look at when direct sun reaches the glass and what can be changed before adding another powered appliance.

Check before buying

  • Map which window gets direct sun at the time you use the room.
  • Check the fixing surface, dimensions and manufacturer's wind limitations for any external shade.
  • For a rental, check required permission before permanent fixings. Keep exits and existing ventilation usable.
  • Window planning: include shade, closing and daily use
