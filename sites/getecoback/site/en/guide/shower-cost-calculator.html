Defaults are illustrative scenarios, not current tariffs or measured savings. Use one currency for every amount; no exchange-rate conversion. Units are litres, °C and kWh.

Method and example

Heat = litres × temperature rise × 0.001163 kWh; input energy = heat ÷ system factor. Add variable water and wastewater cost. Example: 12 litres/minute × 8 minutes = 96 litres; 10 to 38 °C requires 3.126144 kWh of heat. With factor 1, energy at 0.35/kWh and water at 5/m³, cost is about 1.57 per shower; at 8 litres/minute, about 1.05.

Measure what comes out

If safe, collect water at your normal shower setting for a timed interval. Litres collected divided by seconds, multiplied by 60, gives litres per minute. Avoid burns and slippery floors. Repeat the measurement: supply pressure and settings affect it. A nominal product flow is not necessarily the flow in your bathroom.

Use the right energy factor

Heat demand equals litres × temperature rise × 0.001163 kWh. Divide by useful heat delivered per unit of input energy. A factor of 1 is an illustrative direct-heating case, not a measured system efficiency. Use an appropriate factor for the actual equipment; hot-water heat-pump performance is not necessarily its space-heating rating. Distribution and storage losses are excluded.

Check compatibility before reducing flow

Some instantaneous water heaters require minimum flow, and low-pressure arrangements may restrict which fittings can be used. Read both showerhead and heater instructions. Do not lower stored-water temperature or alter hygiene cycles because this calculator uses a lower mixed shower temperature; those are different settings.

Compare variable costs honestly

Enter only the variable water, wastewater and energy rates in one currency. Standing charges remain outside the calculation. Equal duration and mixed-water temperature are assumed. If a lower flow makes you shower longer, update the minutes too. Payback does not include installation, maintenance or financing.

Common questions

Does mixed shower temperature set the storage tank?

No. Mixed outlet temperature and safe hot-water storage are different. Follow system hygiene requirements.

Can a water-saving head fit any heater?

No. Minimum-flow and fitting requirements must be checked for the actual system.

Method & boundaries

Label: kWh/100 cycles ÷ 100 × yearly cycles; annual labels unchanged. Total = purchase + annual electricity × rate × years. Payback = positive extra investment ÷ positive annual saving. Dishes: machine energy × electricity price + litres × water price/1000; price handwash heating separately.

Annual labels retain their standard test usage; cycle counts affect only cycle-based labels. Do not compare different test standards or capacities. Costs exclude finance, repairs, water, residual value and replacement. Keeping or repairing may be better. Electric appliances only: gas-appliance running costs are not modelled.

Select the energy boundary: whole-cycle electricity includes heating once; for a hot-feed machine, use appliance-only electricity and externally heated litres separately. Do not enter annual EnergyGuide kWh as cycle energy. Dishwashers use the same load and rinse volume. The comparison excludes detergent, cleaning quality, time, purchase and installation costs.

Water difference = flow difference × minutes × showers. Input energy = litres × temperature rise × 0.001163 ÷ heat/input ratio. Heat-pump ratio can exceed 1; use fuel price for a boiler. Does not model fixed tank losses. Do not change stored-water safety settings.

Before buying

  • Heat-pump dryers: check capacity, space, drainage and cycle duration.
  • Fridges and freezers: compare usable volume, ventilation clearance and annual kWh.
  • Dishwashers: match load size, eco programme, plumbing and installation dimensions.
  • Low-flow showerheads: check minimum flow, pressure, heater and approval; shorter showers remain a no-purchase option.
