## Start with the actual room conditions
A compressor dehumidifier condenses water on a cold coil; a desiccant model removes water using an adsorbent and regeneration process. Temperature and humidity affect performance, but neither a 10°C nor a 15°C room-temperature threshold proves which is the better buy.

The dew point tells you the temperature below which condensation can occur. It does not tell you the coil temperature, defrost time, extraction rate or cost of a particular appliance. At 10°C and 60% RH, the dew point is about 2.6°C: a coil below that value need not be below zero.

## What to compare before buying

- Find each model’s operating range and extraction at the same temperature and humidity.
- Compare power together with water extraction; lower hourly watts do not guarantee a cheaper completed task.
- Check noise at the intended setting, drainage, tank access and placement instructions.
- If humidity remains high, investigate moisture sources and ventilation rather than assuming the device is too small.

## Common questions

### Does a dew point above zero mean the compressor coil must freeze?
No. A coil must be below the dew point to condense water, but that can still be above freezing. The model and its control strategy matter.

### Can this calculator pick the cheaper technology?
No. Compare condition-matched extraction, input power and complete-session cost for the exact models, within their operating ranges.
