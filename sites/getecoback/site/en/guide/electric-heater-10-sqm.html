How many watts for 10 m²?

For 10 m², 600–800 W is usually enough. Well-insulated rooms need less, old buildings more. Well-insulated new buildings sit at the lower end of the 60–100 W per m² band, old buildings with high ceilings at the upper end.

What does heating 10 m² cost?

At full power, 700 W draws about €0.21 per hour (calculated at €0.30/kWh — put in your own tariff). For a season: with 5 heating hours a day, a thermostat duty cycle of about 40 % and 150 heating days that is roughly €63 per season. The formula is transparent: watts ÷ 1000 × price × hours × duty cycle × days. Electric resistance heating turns one kilowatt-hour of electricity into exactly one kilowatt-hour of heat — that physics is why a heat pump beats it for whole-home, all-winter heating.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts of heating power do I need for 10 m²?
As a rule of thumb 600–800 W (around 60–100 W per m², depending on insulation). For 10 m², 600–800 W is usually enough. Well-insulated rooms need less, old buildings more.

What does heating with 700 W cost?
At full power a 700 W heater draws about €0.21 per hour (at €0.30/kWh). A thermostat lowers that considerably, because it does not heat continuously.

What does a heating season cost for 10 m²?
With 700 W, 5 heating hours a day, a thermostat duty cycle of about 40 % and 150 heating days you end up at roughly €63 per season (at €0.30/kWh). Recalculate with your own tariff: watts ÷ 1000 × price × hours × duty cycle × days.

Is an electric panel heater enough as the only heating for 10 m²?
As a supplementary or transition-season heater, yes. As the sole heating in deep winter, a building heat pump is usually more economical — electric resistance heating turns every kilowatt-hour of expensive electricity into exactly one kilowatt-hour of heat, no more.
