## What heats a room up — the factors

The check scores the four factors from 0 to 100. The higher the total, the higher the overheating risk:

Factor | Biggest driver | Effect |

Location | Attic / loft | strongest — roof rooms heat up extremely |

Window direction | South / West | very strong — direct afternoon sun |

Window area | large / floor-to-ceiling | strong — more glass, more solar gain |

Insulation | old building / poor | medium — heat stays in the room longer |

## What helps — in the right order

Order matters: **first block the sun, then ventilate, then actively cool.** An air conditioner in an unshaded south-facing attic room fights the incoming sun — with external shading or a thermal blind the cooling demand drops sharply. Only then does the risk decide whether a fan, an air cooler or a portable air conditioner is the answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my room get so hot?**The biggest factors are location (attic and top floors heat up the most), window direction (south/west lets in the most sun), large window areas and poor insulation. An attic room with a big south window in an old building has the highest risk.

**What helps most against an overheated room?**In this order: first block the sun (external or a thermal blind), then ventilate at night and early, then actively cool — a fan at low, an air cooler at medium, a portable air conditioner at high risk.

**Do I need an air conditioner in an attic room?**Often yes: attic rooms heat up the most. The sensible combination is shading plus a portable air conditioner with enough BTU — size one step up for an attic.
