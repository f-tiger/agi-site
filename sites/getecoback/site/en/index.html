Which cooler should you actually buy?

A 2-minute guide for the 2026 heatwave. In much of Western Europe, high installation costs and long wait times mean a no-installation unit you can order today often makes the most sense.

1. You want real cooling in one room → portable air conditioner

A portable AC (like the popular Midea PortaSplit style) is a genuine air conditioner: it compresses refrigerant and pumps hot air out through a window kit. It's the only no-installation option that truly drops the temperature, not just moves air around. Expect €250–600 depending on BTU and whether it also heats. Look for at least 9,000 BTU for a bedroom, 12,000+ for a living room.

Compare portable air conditioners on Amazon.de →

2. You have a big or open-plan room → mobile / monobloc AC

For larger or open-plan spaces, a higher-BTU mobile unit on wheels does the job. These cost a bit more (€300+) but cool spaces a small portable can't keep up with. Check the room-size rating (m²) before buying and make sure you can route the exhaust hose to a window.

See mobile AC units on Amazon.de →

3. You're on a budget or renting → air cooler or fan

If you can't fit a window exhaust or don't want to spend €250+, an evaporative air cooler (from ~€60) or a good tower fan (from ~€30) gives real relief — especially in drier heat. They won't lower the room temperature like an AC, but they're cheap, quiet, and need zero installation. Perfect for renters who can't drill or vent.

Air coolers on Amazon.de →  ·  Tower fans →

4. Your flat feels muggy, not just hot → dehumidifier

Humidity is why 28°C can feel unbearable. In damp ground-floor flats, a dehumidifier (from ~€120) can make the air feel noticeably cooler and helps you sleep, often for less power than an AC. Worth it if your home traps moisture.
