FAQ

How much is the tariff on packages from China to the US in 2026?

As reported in 2026 trade guides, small shipments from China that once entered duty-free under the $800 de minimis rule now face roughly a 54% ad-valorem duty or a $100 flat fee per shipment on courier routes, while postal-network parcels carry flat duties reported between $80 and $200 per item depending on origin. Formal entries stack the product's base tariff with Section 301 and 2025-26 additions.

Is the $800 de minimis exemption still available?

No. De minimis treatment was suspended for China and Hong Kong in May 2025 and extended to all origins in February 2026, so there is no longer a value threshold under which imports enter the US duty-free.

What is the cheapest way to import small quantities from China now?

For most products, consolidating into fewer, larger shipments and entering formally with a customs broker now beats many small parcels, because flat per-item duties punish low-value packages hardest. Comparing the courier ad-valorem route against postal flat duties for your specific order value — which the calculator above does — is the first check.

Are these numbers official rates?

They are the figures reported by the 2026 trade and logistics guides cited on this page, current as of August 2026. Tariff policy has changed repeatedly since early 2025; always confirm the live rate with a licensed customs broker or CBP before wiring money.
