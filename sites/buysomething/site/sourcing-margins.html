Method, and what these numbers are not

  • Source. Every figure is computed from this site’s curated pick dataset — the factory price bands, retail bands, MOQs, tariff rates and per-unit freight published on each pick card. The page is regenerated by script from that dataset, so it cannot drift from it.
  • These are researched figures, not audited invoices. Factory bands come from listed 1688 and Alibaba pricing at curation time; Western retail is an observed price band, not a guaranteed selling price.
  • Gross, not net. The multiples exclude advertising, returns, platform commission, storage, samples and inspection. On thin categories those costs consume the entire spread.
  • Tariffs move. The duty rates baked into each pick were current at curation; policy has changed repeatedly since 2025. Confirm live rates with a customs broker before committing money.
  • Sample of 31. This is what our own curated set shows. It is not a claim about every product category in the world, and we will not present it as one.

Frequently asked questions

What margin do you actually get importing from China?

On this site's own 31 curated picks, the honest answer is a range, not a number. Taking the worst case on every input — the top of the factory price band, plus the US tariff, plus per-unit freight, compared against the LOWEST Western retail price — the median multiple is 1.9× and 17 of 31 picks come in under 2×. Taking the best case on every input — the bottom of the factory band against the highest retail price — the median is 7.9×. Both are computed from the same published dataset; which end you land on is decided by your buy price and your selling price, not by the category.

Is the '10x margin from China' claim true?

It exists only at the optimistic end of the range. Across these 31 picks the optimistic multiple runs from 2.6× to 28.4×, so a 10× outcome is real for some products under best-case buying and pricing — but the same products land at 1.1× to 4.2× under worst-case assumptions, and the conservative median across the whole set is 1.9×. Any single headline multiple quoted without stating which end of both price bands it uses is not checkable.

How much money do you need for a first order?

MOQ multiplied by the top of the factory price band gives a median first-order goods cost of $3,300 across these picks, ranging from $690 to $7,200 before freight, duty, inspection and samples. That is goods only; the landed-cost page covers what customs adds on top.

Does harder compliance buy you more margin?

Not in this dataset. Grouping the picks by the compliance difficulty already recorded for each one, the conservative median multiple is low: 2.3×, medium: 1.7×, high: 2.3×. In other words, taking on the harder certification burden does not, by itself, show up as a wider spread here — the compliance work buys defensibility against competitors, not a better buy price.

Where do these numbers come from?

They are computed from this site's own curated pick dataset — the same 1688 and Alibaba factory price bands, Western retail bands, MOQs, US tariff rates and per-unit freight figures published on each pick card, recomputed by a script whenever the dataset changes. They are the curator's researched figures, not audited invoices, and Western retail is an observed price band rather than a guaranteed selling price. Check any individual row against its pick card, and confirm live duty rates with a customs broker before wiring money.
