Find the doll that fits your actual life

Six questions shops hope you never ask yourself — starting with the one behind most buyer regret: weight. It ends on one of four outcomes — a torso, a compact build, full-size TPE, or full-size silicone — and each one says why it fits you and what it costs you to get it wrong.

Why does this ask about weight before it asks about looks?

Every shop in this category sorts by appearance, because appearance is what sells. Ask owners what they actually regret and you get a different answer almost every time: the weight. A full-size doll is 55 to 100+ lb of limp, unbalanced dead weight, and unlike a barbell it cannot help you carry it. You will lift it to reposition it, carry it to clean it, and move it in and out of storage — not once, but for as long as you own it. People who bought on looks and discovered the weight afterwards are the ones whose dolls end up in a closet.

So the finder asks the uncomfortable question first, then budget, then upkeep, then storage. The order is deliberate: each answer constrains the next, and the constraints that are hardest to change come first. You cannot decide to be stronger next month; you can decide to spend more. And the number to test against is the stated weight of the actual doll, not a guess from its height — we charted real listings and height barely predicts it.

What do the four outcomes mean?

  • Premium torso (13–30 lb for most, roughly $150–$700). A partial body — no legs, sometimes no arms. Fits in a drawer or a small case, needs no storage plan, and costs a fifth of a full doll. This is the recommendation when your weight tolerance, storage or budget answers say a full-size doll would become a burden. It is also the cheapest honest way to find out whether you enjoy ownership at all.
  • Compact doll (roughly 140–155 cm, 50–60 lb, $600–$1,300). A full body at a manageable weight. Easier to move, store and dress than a flagship, and materially cheaper. The usual answer for someone who wants the whole form but was honest about the lifting.
  • Full-size TPE ($800–$1,800). The softest material, the lowest price per inch, and a real maintenance commitment: cleaning after use, powdering monthly, oiling every one to three months. Recommended when you have the strength and space and said yes to the upkeep question. TPE is porous, so thorough drying is not optional.
  • Full-size platinum silicone ($1,800–$3,500). Firmer feel, the best facial detail, non-porous and genuinely easy to keep hygienic, and the longest lifespan. Costs more to buy and less to own. Recommended when budget allows and you were honest that a monthly routine is not going to happen.

What does this finder deliberately not do?

It does not name a specific product, because it cannot see current stock, and a recommendation that points at a sold-out listing is worse than no recommendation. It does not ask for your email — the result appears immediately and the page never gates it. And it does not send your answers anywhere: the scoring runs entirely in your browser, and the shareable link opens the finder rather than your result.

What it will not save you from is the vendor. A correct category and a counterfeit shop still ends badly, so run the 10-point Scam-Check on whoever you are about to pay, and put the real total through the True Cost Calculator before you commit — duties, care supplies and storage typically add 15–25% to the sticker price.

Common questions

Is a torso really "80% of the experience"? That figure is our editorial judgement, not a measurement. What is not in dispute is the ratio of cost, weight and storage burden, which is closer to one-fifth. Owners who upgrade later rarely regret having started small; owners who started large and found it unmanageable regret it often.

Does material change the weight? Barely. Silicone is slightly denser, but a full-size doll is heavy in either material. If weight is your constraint, the fix is size, not material.

What about a hybrid? A silicone head on a TPE body gets you the best facial realism and durable makeup at a TPE body price. It is a genuinely good middle path and most factories offer it — see the material comparison.
