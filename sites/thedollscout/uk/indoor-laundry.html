Drying clothes indoors in the UK: moisture, space and running cost

Decide whether you need ventilation, a humidity monitor or a dehumidifier. Compare whole-session electricity use and local model details before buying.

Editorial sources checked: 2026-11-15 · TDS editorial method (English)

Start here

A dehumidifier and an air purifier do different jobs. For laundry, start with the moisture source, ventilation and available drying space. If you compare dehumidifiers, use performance at similar temperature and humidity and the cost of a whole drying session.

How to choose

Find the problem before choosing an appliance

Note when moisture appears, where laundry dries and whether extraction and ventilation work as intended. A purchase does not repair a leak or a building defect. For persistent damp, follow appropriate housing or professional advice rather than relying on a product guide.

Compare the same conditions

Which? describes testing extraction, energy use and noise. Use that decision framework, but do not treat TDS as a testing laboratory. A headline litres-per-day claim is not a promise for your room, and tank volume is a different measurement.

Budget for the full job

Use measured kWh for a complete comparable session multiplied by your own GBP-per-kWh tariff. Rated watts × hours ÷ 1,000 is only a constant-power estimate; cycling and conditions change. Add filter costs only where the chosen mode actually needs that filter.

Before buying

  1. Confirm the exact generation, UK plug, rated supply and manufacturer operating range.
  2. Compare a whole session under similar conditions; do not compare only maximum watts.
  3. Check the standard washable filter versus optional replacement filters in the exact manual.
  4. Verify delivery to your postcode, current stock, seller, warranty and return arrangements.

Questions before choosing

Does the lowest wattage mean the cheapest drying?

No. A lower-power appliance may need longer. Compare a similar load and drying outcome over the whole session, using your own tariff.

Is a small moisture absorber equivalent to a room dehumidifier?

No. Check the intended space and extraction method. A product intended for a cupboard is not evidence of useful performance for wet laundry in a room.
