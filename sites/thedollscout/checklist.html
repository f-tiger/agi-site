The first-time buyer checklist

Everything to check before you spend $1,000–$3,500, in the order that prevents regret. Print it, tick it as you go, keep it with your order confirmation.

No email required. Other sites put this behind a signup form. We didn't, because a checklist that stops people getting defrauded is worth more to us circulating freely than sitting in a mailing list. Not sure what to buy yet? 60-second finder

1 · Before you shop

  • Do the weight test. Carry the weight class you're considering up your stairs — 55–100+ lb for a full-size doll. Do this before you fall in love with a face. Weight is the single most reported regret.
  • Set an all-in budget, not a sticker budget. Add 15–25% for factory add-ons, care supplies, storage and import charges.
  • Answer one material question honestly: will you genuinely keep up a monthly maintenance routine? Yes → TPE. No → silicone, or wait until you can afford it.
  • Decide where it will live before it arrives. A standard closet rod bends under a full-size doll.
  • Check your country's import rules. Legality, tax and seizure risk vary enormously.

2 · Vetting the shop (before you even pick a doll)

  • Pricing is realistic. No 50%+ discounts, no "$799 full-size silicone", no permanent countdown timers. A discount that big is the scam signal, not the bargain.
  • Brand authorisation is verifiable. The shop names the factory, and that factory confirms the shop is an authorised reseller when you email them.
  • Factory-photo policy is in writing. Photos of your doll before dispatch, shipped only on your approval.
  • Buyer-protected payment is accepted. PayPal or a credit card. Wire, crypto or gift cards only means walk away.
  • Reviews read organically, including complaints that got resolved. Walls of invited five-star reviews don't count.
  • There's a real contact surface — an address and a human who answers clearly about lead times and customs.
  • Lead times are honest. Made-to-order means 2–8 weeks. Everything "in stock, ships today" at factory prices is drop-shipping fiction.
  • Photos survive a reverse-image search and match the official factory catalogue.
  • Policies are published before checkout — shipping, customs, cancellation, defects.
  • The domain has history. Check any WHOIS lookup. Scam shops churn new domains constantly.

3 · Before you pay

  • Ask: is this price DDP or DDU? Delivered Duty Paid means import charges are covered. Delivered Duty Unpaid means the carrier will bill you before release. A vendor who can't answer clearly is telling you something.
  • Never ask the seller to under-declare the value. It's customs fraud, it's the most common bad advice in this hobby, and the saving is not proportionate to the exposure.
  • Confirm the billing descriptor that will appear on your card statement.
  • Confirm the packaging is unmarked and what the shipping label will say.
  • Screenshot everything — the listing, the configuration, the price, the promises. Before payment, not after a dispute starts.
  • Pay with PayPal or a credit card. This is the step that gives you leverage later.

4 · After you order, before it ships

  • Silence during production is normal. Expect 1–4 weeks with no news.
  • Demand the factory photos and actually study them. Face, body, skin tone, every option you paid for, against your order sheet. This is your last easy exit.
  • Do not approve dispatch until the photos match. This single habit prevents the most-reported complaint in the entire category.
  • Never accept an offer to ship in parts to avoid detection. In more than one prosecution, part-shipping was treated as evidence of guilt.
  • Expect tracking to stall at customs for days. That's normal, not a sign of fraud.

5 · Arrival day

  • Film the unboxing — continuous video from sealed crate to unwrapped doll. This is the evidence that wins disputes.
  • Note any crate damage with the courier at delivery if you can.
  • Run the factory authenticity check if the brand has one, on day one, while your dispute window is fresh.
  • Confirm every option you paid for is actually present.
  • Photograph any defect immediately and raise it in writing the same day.

6 · If something is wrong

  • Document first, negotiate second. Vendors who stall with "please wait, remake coming" are running out your dispute clock.
  • PayPal dispute: you have 180 days from payment. "Item significantly not as described" fits a counterfeit exactly.
  • Credit card chargeback with the same evidence.
  • Never trade a five-star review for a repair. That's review extortion, and you lose your leverage the moment you accept.
  • Report the shop and warn other buyers.
