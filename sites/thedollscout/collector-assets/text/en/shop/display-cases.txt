Choose between an enclosed case, an open riser and the shelf you already own. Inside dimensions and access matter more than a claimed figure count.

What to measure

Measure the widest point, depth and total height of each figure, including its base and projecting parts. A series name is not a size specification.

Measure usable inside width, depth and height. Subtract hinges, door travel and shelf supports; leave room to put a hand in and lift the figure out.

For a riser, check every tier’s depth, height and support surface. The tallest figure on the top tier must still clear the enclosure.

Compare the options

Enclosed case — You want a barrier against routine dust and accidental contact. — Doors and joints are not a dust-proof guarantee. Check inside measurements, access and closure.

Open riser — You want to see short figures behind the front row. — Figures remain exposed. Check tier depth and stability with the intended base.

Existing shelf — The pieces already fit and are easy to reach. — Rearranging may solve the problem without buying a case. Confirm the shelf’s load limit.

Before paying

Compare internal measurements rather than the headline outside dimensions. Ask the seller if only the outside size is given.

Check stated load limits, assembly, wall-anchoring instructions and the intended support surface. A fit calculation does not prove stability or safety.

Check whether risers, shelves and fixing hardware are included. Compare the delivered total and the return cost for bulky items.

When to skip the purchase

If the usable inside measurements are unknown, a lid cannot clear the tallest piece, or the shelf is already stable and sufficient, pause the purchase. “Fits 20 figures” is not a substitute for dimensions.
