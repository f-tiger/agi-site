## One line

A practical, printable page: **"How to verify factory photos before you pay"**
— the reader asks the vendor for specific photos and checks them against the
site's own spec datasets. It converts the site's unique machine-readable asset
(doll-specs.json) into a buyer action no competitor page offers.

## Verified evidence (the three gates)

1. **Data gate**: the trust cluster (scam-check, payment-protection) is what AI
   engines already cite daily (crawl-log.json). This page extends that cluster;
   it is not a new bet.
2. **Demand gate**: Trustpilot reviews across MULTIPLE vendor stores show the
   same recurring complaint pattern — "product not as pictured" / resold stock
   photos / wrong body or head on arrival. That is a verified cross-store
   pattern, not one angry customer. Search surface: "how to verify sex doll
   factory photos" and adjacent queries return almost no practical tutorial —
   a genuine gap. **Honesty rule: do not claim to be "the first" such guide;
   if a decent one is later found, link it and differentiate instead.**
3. **Business gate**: every checklist step ends where the money is — verified
   vendors (affiliate deep links) who agree to send factory photos, plus the
   scam-check tool for everything else. The page funnels distrust into the
   site's two conversion surfaces.

## Page structure (build spec)

All checks must be things a buyer can actually do from a photo; zero fabricated
forensics. Four verification moves, each one section:

1. **Spec cross-check**: ask for a tape-measure photo; compare height /
   bust-waist-hip / weight against the model's row in the site's spec dataset
   (link the exact model page). A factory photo of a different body shows up as
   a measurement mismatch — this is the check only this site can power.
2. **Natural-light skin tone**: request one photo by a window in daylight.
   Studio lighting hides tone mismatch between ordered and shipped skin color —
   the most common "not as pictured" complaint in the Trustpilot pattern.
3. **Pose test = skeleton test**: request one specific non-catalog pose
   (e.g. one arm raised, seated). A resold stock photo cannot comply; a real
   factory photo of your unit can. Also verifies the articulation actually
   ordered.
4. **Same-frame reference object**: ask for a dated note or common object
   (soda can) in frame — defeats re-sent catalog shots and confirms the photo
   is of the unit being shipped, with correct scale.

Plus: a copy-paste message template the buyer sends the vendor requesting
exactly these four photos (EN); a "what a refusal tells you" paragraph
(links scam-check); printable / checkbox format consistent with checklist.html;
FAQPage JSON-LD matching visible Q&As verbatim; wired into llms.txt, sitemap,
and internal links from scam-check.html, checklist.html, after-you-order.html.
