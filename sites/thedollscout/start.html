Your first Labubu, bought well.

A first Labubu is three decisions, made in the right order — format, series, channel. Get those right and everything else on this site is just backup. Last updated 2026-08-30.

The short answer: choose the format you'll actually use (the vinyl-plush pendant is the classic bag charm), a series whose openly listed regulars you'd be happy with — at 1:72, your first box will almost certainly not hold the secret — and a channel where Pop Mart is the seller (the ranked list). Wanting one specific figure? Buy it opened, at a known price, through the checklist — not through luck.

Decision 1 — the format

FormatWhat it isGood first buy if…
Vinyl plush pendant (blind box)Soft plush body, vinyl face, loop for a bag — the dominant Labubu form and the one on every bag you've seen…you want the classic charm and the opening thrill

The format is printed on the official listing, and it matters beyond taste: fur evenness and face finish are two of the eight authenticity checks.

Decision 2 — the series

A series gives you six or twelve openly listed regular designs plus one hidden secret at long odds (the mechanics). The beginner's rule follows directly from the math: pick a series whose regulars you'd genuinely be happy to pull — the lineup art on the box is what you're realistically buying. If a series only appeals to you for its secret, that's not a first buy, that's a chase — read the cost page first.

The current series, linked to their official listings, live on our homepage — the live Pop Mart page is always the reference for what a series contains and costs.

Decision 3 — the channel

The channel decides whether the printed odds are the odds you actually get, and whether the figure is real at all. The full ranked list is on where to buy; the beginner's version: official Pop Mart channels first (store, app, official web shop), marketplace listings only where Pop Mart is the seller of record — the “sold by” line, not the site's logo.

The first-buy checklist

  • Set the number first. One box is a complete first experience. Decide any more while calm, not at the shelf.
  • Check the price floor. A brand-new “authentic” figure far below official retail is the classic counterfeit signal — the too-good listing is answered by the checklist, not by luck.
  • Sold out ≠ available cheap somewhere. Hot series genuinely sell out; a sold-out figure that's suddenly in stock, cheap and “guaranteed” is the fake market working exactly as designed. Patience or the opened-figure path — never the shortcut.
  • Keep the box. Box finish and the anti-counterfeit seal are checks you can run after the fact — and matter if you ever pass the figure on.

For parents

  • Age fit: check the age recommendation on the official listing for the specific item — formats differ, and Pop Mart's own listing is the authority. Where their current guidance disagrees with anything here, follow theirs.
  • Safety and fakes: counterfeits bypass the entire safety and QC chain — and the fake market concentrates on exactly the hot figures kids ask for. Official channels aren't just about authenticity; they're the only path where the safety chain exists at all.
  • The mechanism, honestly: blind boxes run on the same unpredictable-reward psychology as games of chance (explained with sources here). That's worth knowing before it's in a kid's routine: agree the box count together, before the store.

Quick answers

The same questions, answered in one paragraph each — the versions an assistant can quote without losing the caveats.

Which Labubu should I buy first?

Pick by three decisions in order: format (the vinyl-plush pendant is the classic bag-charm form; all-vinyl figures and larger plushes exist too), series (choose one whose openly listed regular designs you would genuinely be happy with — at 1:72 your first box will almost certainly not hold the secret), and channel (an official Pop Mart channel or a marketplace listing where Pop Mart itself is the seller of record). The current series and their official listings are linked from our homepage.

Should my first buy be a blind box or a known figure?

Buy the blind box if what excites you is the opening — that thrill is the product, and one box is a cheap ticket to it. Buy a known, opened figure if what you want is one specific design: certainty at a known price is usually cheaper than chasing it through blind boxes, but it moves the risk from luck to authenticity, so the 8-point check and the seller of record matter instead.

Is Labubu OK for kids? What should parents know?

Three things. First, check the age recommendation on the official product listing for the specific item — formats differ, and Pop Mart's own listing is the authority. Second, buy through official channels: counterfeits bypass the entire safety and quality-control chain, and the fake market targets exactly the hot figures kids ask for. Third, know that blind boxes run on the same unpredictable-reward psychology as games of chance — decide the number of boxes with your kid before buying, not at the shelf.

Why is the one I want always sold out?

Hot series genuinely sell out fast at official channels, and that scarcity is what the counterfeit market feeds on: a sold-out figure suddenly available cheap, in stock and 'guaranteed authentic' is the classic fake pattern our price-floor logic flags. The honest options are patience for a restock at an official channel, or buying opened from a marketplace listing that passes the checklist — never the too-good listing.
