Real vs fake Labubu: the 8-point check.

Counterfeit Labubus are common enough to have earned their own name — Lafufu (Gulf News). Below are the checks that recur across collector guides and press coverage of Pop Mart's own advice, each with its source named. Recorded 2026-08-30; counterfeits improve, so newer guidance from Pop Mart outranks this page.

The short answer: count the teeth first — a real Labubu has exactly nine, distinct, with gaps. Then the face (soft peach, airbrushed blush, glossy eyes), the box (matte, embossed, muted colours), the anti-counterfeit QR seal (check it lands on popmart.com, not a lookalike), and the price (far below retail = the counterfeit band). And the check that outranks all of these: buy where fakes structurally can't reach you.

How many teeth does a real Labubu have?

Nine. The nine-teeth count is the most widely reported single check, cited both in collector guides and in press coverage of Pop Mart's own advice: authentic figures have exactly nine distinct teeth with visible gaps between them, while counterfeits often have the wrong count, fused teeth, or a single sloppy white blob. Count them before anything else — it is the fastest check that requires no packaging.

Does a real Labubu box have a QR code?

Recent authentic packaging carries an anti-counterfeit seal with a QR code that should resolve to Pop Mart's own verification. Two cautions reported by collector guides: counterfeiters also print QR stickers, so what matters is not that a code exists but where it leads — check that the domain you land on is popmart.com, not a lookalike. And an older or re-sealed box is a question in itself. We could not verify the exact seal design for every series and year, so treat the seal as one check among eight, not a verdict.

What does a fake Labubu (Lafufu) usually get wrong?

The recurring giveaways reported across sources: the wrong tooth count or fused teeth; a harsh pink or orange face where the authentic tone is a soft peach with airbrushed blush; flat dull eyes without the glossy depth; glossy or washed-out packaging where authentic boxes are matte with embossed series details; rough seams, uneven fur, and light hollow-feeling plastic; and a price far below official retail. No single flaw is proof by itself — counterfeits improve constantly — which is why the seller check outranks all cosmetic checks.

Is a cheap Labubu ever real?

Treat deep discounts on “new authentic” stock as a counterfeit signal. Tracked retail for standard blind boxes runs roughly $14 to $28 (DemandSage's 2026 statistics roundup), with some premium series listed higher on Pop Mart's own store. Sold-out series trade above retail on the secondary market — which is exactly why a brand-new listing far below retail makes no commercial sense for a genuine item. The one honest exception: official sales on slower series do exist, on official channels. Off official channels, the discount is the product.

What can this checklist not tell you?

  • Whether a listing's photos are of the unit you'll receive. Counterfeit sellers photograph authentic units. The checks above work on the object in your hands; before payment, the only real protection is the channel.
  • Anything about series we haven't covered. Seal designs, markings and box details vary by series and year; where our sources didn't cover a series, we say so rather than extrapolate.
  • Tomorrow's fakes. Every public checklist — this one included — is also a to-do list for counterfeiters. If Pop Mart's current guidance disagrees with this page, follow Pop Mart's.
