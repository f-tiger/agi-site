What a doll actually costs — from 50 live listings, and one claim we had to withdraw

Last updated 11 August 2026 · every number sourced, or marked unverified

Every price guide in this category gives a range like "$100 to $10,000", which is true and useless. We record the price, height and weight of every doll our scraper can reach at a vetted vendor, and band what comes back. We have now done it twice. The second time contradicted the first, so this page leads with that.

We were wrong, and here is the correction. On 27 July 2026 we recorded 33 full-size listings, found the cheapest at $1,099, and wrote that no full-size doll costs under $1,000. On 11 August 2026 the same scraper reached 50 full-size listings and found two at $899 — the same 150 cm F-cup TPE model, held in US warehouse stock. The claim was true of what we had seen and false of the market. It is withdrawn. What survives is the part that matters: the real floor is around $899, and the "$299 full-size premium doll" you saw advertised is still nowhere near it.

Why we published the correction instead of the better number

The 27 July figure was not a lie and it was not a mistake in arithmetic. It was a claim about a market made from a sample of one vendor's catalogue on one day, and it did not survive contact with a second day. Almost every guide in this category would have quietly updated the number, or simply left the older, tidier claim standing.

We publish the dataset, so leaving it standing was never available to us — and that is the entire argument for publishing the dataset. A number you cannot check is a number you have to trust; a number you can check is one we cannot quietly move. Both recording dates are kept, and the prices on this page carry the date they were seen, because a price without a date is not evidence about anything.

Why the empty band matters more than the full ones

Factories set minimum pricing, and legitimate vendors sit roughly 10–15% below factory list. That is why the space between "torso money" and "$1,099" contains nothing at a vetted shop — the economics of licensed manufacture do not reach there. But marketplace and social-media listings advertise that band heavily, because it is exactly where an impulse purchase lives: enough to feel premium, little enough to skip the research.

What ships from that band, when anything ships at all, is a counterfeit cast from stolen photos, or an inflatable product photographed as a premium doll. This is not an inference from pricing theory; it is a recurring pattern in documented buyer complaints — the same reports our Scam-Check was built from. If a listing claims full-size under $1,000, run it through the 10-point Scam-Check before paying — and if it fails, the answer was free.

The sticker is not the cost

Whatever band you buy in, the first year adds to it. Care supplies run differently by material (TPE needs more), storage is real money if you do it properly, and import charges depend entirely on your country — the US currently has no duty-free allowance at all, and nobody can honestly quote a US duty rate for a full-size doll because the tariff classification is unsettled.
