How much a doll actually weighs, by height

Last updated 27 July 2026 · every number sourced, or marked unverified

Weight is the most reported source of buyer regret, and almost no shop prints it next to the photo. So we collected it ourselves: 33 full-body and 6 torso listings, vendor-stated figures, gathered 27 July 2026. Method and limitations at the bottom.

The finding that matters: height barely predicts weight — build does. At 150–151 cm alone, the range runs from 43 lb (slim B-cup) to 94 lb (heavy M-cup). A slim 151 cm doll was lighter than every 140 cm doll we found, and a curvy 163 cm doll outweighed a taller 173 cm one. If weight is your constraint, filter by the stated weight, never by height.

What these numbers mean in practice

  • Do the carry test at the real number. Take the stated weight of the doll you're considering — not a guess from its height — and carry that up your stairs. It will be harder than the test, not easier: a doll is limp, unbalanced, and cannot hold on. The 60-Second Finder asks about this before anything else.
  • 60 lb vs 95 lb is a different ownership. The difference decides whether cleaning, repositioning and storage happen casually or become the reason the doll ends up permanently stored.
  • If weight rules out full-size, it doesn't rule out everything. A slim-build full doll (43–63 lb in this sample), a small torso (13–18 lb), or a compact build each solve it differently.
  • A listing with no stated weight is telling you something. Vendors know weight stops sales. Ask for it in writing before you pay; a shop that won't state it fails the spirit of the ten checks.

Method and limitations

  • Source: product listings at one vetted vendor (YourDoll), collected 27 July 2026 by an automated read of 110 product pages. 33 full-body and 6 torso listings carried usable height and weight; the rest were accessories, spare heads or items without stated figures.
  • These are vendor-stated figures. We did not independently weigh anything. Our consistency check: builds that appeared in several listings returned identical figures every time (63 lb ×4, 73 lb ×5, 111 lb ×3, 100 lb ×2), which is what genuine spec-sheet data looks like and scrape noise does not.
  • Exclusions: one clothing item that inherited a doll's dimensions, and two partial-body items whose recorded weight could not be tied to the product itself. Anything with a childlike appearance is excluded from this site categorically.
  • One vendor's catalogue is not the whole market. Factory builds recur across vendors, so the ranges travel reasonably well — but treat them as ranges, not quotes. This page will be re-collected periodically; the date above is the date that matters.
