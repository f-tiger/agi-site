Guides / Terminology

What the words on a doll listing actually mean

Some of these terms carry real information. Others are decoration that sounds technical. Knowing which is which is most of what stops you overpaying.

Doll-listing vocabulary splits three ways. Some terms change what physically arrives — TPE or silicone, gel-filled or not, articulated fingers, standing feet. Some state a fixed fact you can check — height, weight, cup size. And some sound technical while carrying no information at all, which is where the price premium usually hides. This page sorts every common term into one of the three.

What do the material terms mean?

TPE (thermoplastic elastomer)
Soft, stretchy, relatively cheap. Most bodies are TPE. It is porous, which drives everything else about owning it: it cannot be fully sterilised, it must be dried thoroughly after every clean, it wants powdering roughly monthly and a light mineral-oil coat every one to three months, and it tears and stains more easily than silicone.
Platinum silicone (platinum-cure silicone)
Firmer, non-porous, holds fine skin texture and makeup for years, lasts longest, needs no oiling. It is genuinely expensive raw material — which is why a full-size doll advertised as silicone under about $1,000 is either mislabelled TPE or not a real listing.
Hybrid
Silicone head on a TPE body. Best facial realism and durable makeup at close to a TPE body price. A real configuration most factories offer, not a marketing word.
Shell
The skin material itself, as distinct from the skeleton underneath. Used when someone is discussing repairs.
Skeleton
The internal frame. Upgraded skeletons buy wider joint range and sometimes shrugging shoulders. It is also the part that fails quietly: joints loosen with use and are the usual reason an old doll poses badly.

What do the body and configuration terms mean?

Torso
A partial body — no legs, usually no head. 13–30 lb for most (large 140 cm-class: 60+ lb), $150–$700. Full comparison against a full-size doll.
Height (cm)
The most decision-relevant number on any listing, and it is almost always printed below the photo rather than above it. Height predicts weight, storage burden and whether you will still be using the thing in a year.
Standing feet
Bolts in the soles so the doll can stand unsupported. A common paid add-on. It puts the full weight through the ankles, so it is not the storage solution it sounds like.
Gel bust / hollow breast
Alternatives to solid filling. Gel is softer and adds weight; hollow reduces weight. Both are preference, not quality.
Implanted hair vs wig
Implanted is individually rooted, looks better, costs more and cannot be swapped. A wig is removable and replaceable. Most experienced owners prefer wigs for exactly that reason.

What do the buying and shipping terms mean?

Factory photos
Photographs of your specific doll, taken before dispatch, sent for your approval, with shipment held until you approve. The single habit that prevents the most common complaint in this category. Get the policy in writing before you pay.
Made to order · lead time
These are built after you order. Two to eight weeks total is normal. Silence during production is normal. "In stock, ships today" at factory prices describes drop-shipping, not manufacturing.
DDP / DDU
Delivered Duty Paid — import charges are already covered and the checkout number is the number. Delivered Duty Unpaid — the carrier bills you before release. Asking which applies resolves most of the cost uncertainty in one question. Why this matters more since 2025.
De minimis
A duty-free allowance for low-value imports. The US $800 allowance was suspended on 29 August 2025 and indefinitely extended in June 2026; Germany abolished its €150 threshold on 1 July 2026. Guides still describing these as current are out of date.
Authorised reseller
A shop the factory has actually approved to sell its dolls. The claim is worthless unless the factory confirms it — and confirming it means contacting the factory, not reading the shop's own about page.
Anti-counterfeit code
Some factories ship a scratch-card code you verify on the factory's own site. Useful — but only when checked against the factory's website. A certificate issued by the shop proves nothing about the shop.

What do the care and damage terms mean?

Powdering / renewal powder
Dusting dry TPE to stop the surface going tacky. "Renewal powder" is usually cornstarch with a markup. Cornstarch is cornstarch.
Dye transfer
Colour migrating out of dark clothing into TPE. The most common cosmetic damage there is, largely permanent, and entirely preventable by washing new clothes first.
Flat spots
Permanent compression from resting in one position too long. The reason storage method is a real decision rather than an afterthought.

Which technical-sounding words carry no information?

None of these are defined by any standard body, and no vendor can be held to any of them:

  • "Medical grade" silicone — not a grade. Not a certification. It appears on listings because it sounds like one.
  • "Premium" / "luxury" / "ultra-realistic" — no threshold exists for any of them.
  • "Certified authentic", where the certificate comes from the seller. Circular by construction.
  • "Factory direct" — nearly every shop says it. It is unfalsifiable from outside.
  • "Limited stock" with a countdown timer on a made-to-order product. There is no stock to be limited.

Compare that with the things that are checkable: a stated height, a named factory the factory will confirm, a written factory-photo policy, and whether PayPal is accepted. The ten checks are built entirely out of checkable things — that is the whole design.

The pattern worth remembering: vocabulary that cannot be verified from outside the shop is the vocabulary that scam shops use most, because it costs them nothing to say. When a listing is heavy on adjectives and light on numbers, that is itself the signal.
