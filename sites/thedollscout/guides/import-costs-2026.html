Guides / Import Costs in 2026

The $800 duty-free rule is gone. Most doll guides haven't noticed.

If you are reading a buying guide that tells you US imports under $800 arrive duty-free, that guide is describing a rule that stopped applying on 29 August 2025. This is what replaced it — in the US and five other markets — and what it means for the number on your invoice.

The short version. The United States suspended duty-free de minimis treatment for all countries in August 2025 and made the suspension indefinite for non-postal shipments in June 2026. Germany abolished its €150 duty-free threshold on 1 July 2026. New Zealand added a per-consignment levy in April 2026. Thailand is reported to have scrapped its threshold in January 2026. The low-value import loophole is closing worldwide, and doll buyers are squarely in the blast radius because dolls are heavy, expensive, and almost always ship internationally.

What actually changed in the United States

Three separate instruments, in sequence:

DateWhat happened
4 July 2025The One Big Beautiful Bill Act is enacted, statutorily terminating the de minimis exemption with effect from 1 July 2027.
29 August 2025Executive Order 14324 takes effect, suspending duty-free de minimis treatment for all countries ahead of that date. CBP begins rejecting the low-value filing channels.
24 June 2026Interim final rules indefinitely suspend the exemption for merchandise arriving by every mode other than the international postal network. Low-value goods now require a customs entry and are subject to applicable duties, taxes and fees.

Put together: a doll shipped to a US buyer today is dutiable regardless of its value, needs a customs entry, and will usually pick up a carrier brokerage or disbursement fee on top. The old mental model — "under $800 and it just shows up" — is dead.

So what will you pay?

We are not going to quote you a percentage, and you should be suspicious of any guide that does. Here is the honest reason.

The duty rate follows the tariff classification, and the classification for a full-size adult doll is genuinely unsettled. Public CBP rulings have put vibrating devices under the massage-apparatus heading and a non-vibrating plastic article under the general plastics heading. We could not find a ruling covering a full-size doll at all. Chapter 95, which covers dolls and toys, was written for toys and customs authorities have historically resisted putting adult articles there. Until someone obtains a binding ruling, any specific percentage you read is somebody's guess dressed up as a fact.

What you can do is make the number knowable before you commit:

  1. Ask the vendor whether the price is DDP or DDU. Delivered Duty Paid means the seller has already covered import charges and the number you pay at checkout is the number. Delivered Duty Unpaid means the carrier will bill you before release. This single question resolves most of the uncertainty, and a vendor who cannot answer it clearly is telling you something about how they operate.
  2. Ask what tariff code they declare under. A vendor shipping dolls at volume knows the code they use. If they will not tell you, treat that as a flag.
  3. Budget for the carrier's own fee. Brokerage and disbursement charges are separate from duty and are frequently the part that surprises people.
  4. Run the total through the True Cost Calculator so the import charge sits alongside care supplies and storage rather than arriving as a shock.
The trap: do not ask the seller to under-declare. This is the single most common piece of advice on doll forums, and with de minimis gone it is more tempting than ever. It is also customs fraud under 19 U.S.C. § 1592. You are asking a stranger in another jurisdiction to falsify a customs document in your name, on a shipment addressed to your home, in a product category where an inspection can escalate quickly. The saving is a couple of hundred dollars. The exposure is not proportionate.

It is not just the US

MarketWhat changedPractical effect
GermanyThe €150 duty-free threshold was abolished on 1 July 2026, replaced by a flat €3 charge per tariff position for consignments up to €150.Dolls sit well above €150, so they clear under the normal tariff plus 19% import VAT.
New ZealandPer-consignment Goods Management Levies from 1 April 2026, applying even to low-value imports.A small fixed cost on top of the existing 15% GST treatment.
ThailandThe THB 1,500 threshold is reported to have been abolished from 1 January 2026.Academic for this category — the import itself is prohibited there.
CanadaNo change, but a widely misreported rule.The CAD 40/150 CUSMA thresholds only apply to goods from the US or Mexico. For China-origin courier shipments the threshold is CAD 20, meaning everything is assessable. Every factory named on the listings we have recorded is China-based, so this is the path a doll normally takes — but we have not counted the market and do not claim a share.
AustraliaNo change.The AUD 1,000 threshold decides who collects GST, not whether it is owed. Most full-size dolls exceed it and are assessed at the border.

Why this matters more for dolls than for most purchases

Three things stack up. Dolls are expensive, so a percentage-based charge is a real number rather than a rounding error. They are heavy and bulky, so freight is already a large line item and duty is often calculated on a base that includes it. And they almost always ship from overseas factories, so there is no domestic-purchase escape route the way there is for most consumer goods.

The result is that the gap between the sticker price and the delivered cost is wider in this category than in almost any other, at exactly the moment when the rules that used to soften it are being withdrawn. That is why we built a calculator rather than publishing a price list.

If you take one thing from this page: ask the vendor whether the price is DDP or DDU before you pay, and decline every invitation to under-declare. Those two habits cover most of the downside.

Ask the DDP question before you order

The page above is only actionable at one moment: when you are talking to a vendor and can still ask whether the quoted price is DDP (duties paid, no invoice on your doorstep) or DDU (you settle with customs on arrival). Take the question with you.
