Real vs Fake Where to Buy

Don't pay real money for a Lafufu.

Labubu — the snaggle-toothed elf from Kasing Lung's The Monsters, produced under exclusive license by Pop Mart since 2019 (Wikipedia) — became popular enough that the counterfeits got their own name. This site does one job: help you buy a real one. No hype, no resale flipping advice — an authenticity checklist with named sources, and the short list of channels where fakes structurally can't reach you.

The short answer: buy from an official channel and you never need an authenticity check. Buying anywhere else, run the 8-point check — teeth first (a real Labubu has exactly nine), then the box, the anti-counterfeit seal, and the price. A brand-new “authentic” Labubu far below official retail is not a deal; it is the counterfeit band announcing itself.

Why does this site exist?

Because the failure mode is asymmetric. A fake sneaker disappoints; a fake blind box means you paid blind-box money for an object with none of the thing you were actually buying — the licensed character, the series artwork, the resale floor, the point. And the counterfeit supply is large and professional enough that the community named it: a fake Labubu is a Lafufu (Gulf News). Pop Mart's own advice, relayed through press coverage, is the same as ours: buy through official channels (ABC News).

Who makes Labubu?

Labubu was created by Hong Kong-born artist Kasing Lung for his illustrated series The Monsters (2015). Lung signed an exclusive licensing agreement with Pop Mart in 2019, which produces the vinyl-plush pendants and figures sold in blind boxes today.
