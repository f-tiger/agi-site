How DollScout works — and how we're paid

Because "trusted vendor" lists in this industry are usually pay-to-play, here is our entire model, in the open.

The standard that defines this site: our dataset is published in full, and every number is sourced, or the page says plainly that it could not be verified. No composite scores, no invented percentages, no "industry estimates". When we get something wrong we correct it in public and say we did — we have already done this once, for our own torso-weight figure. Competitors can copy our pages; they cannot copy this without deleting their own fabrications first.

Why is it so hard to tell a real doll seller from a scam?

The adult doll market has a broken trust layer. Counterfeit shops sell knock-offs with stolen factory photos. Review platforms are contaminated by paid reviews and review extortion. Forum "approved vendor" lists are funded by the vendors they approve. Affiliate blogs rank whoever pays the highest commission. Buyers spending $1,000–$3,500 are left guessing.

DollScout's answer: publish the vetting criteria, apply them in public, disclose the money, and include criticism of vendors we recommend.

How does this site make money?

  • When you buy through our links, the vendor pays us a referral commission. It never costs you extra.
  • Current affiliate relationships: yourdoll.com; Amazon Associates (accessory picks).
  • What we don't do: paid placements, sponsored "reviews," selling your email, or letting a commission rate influence a recommendation. A vendor that fails vetting stays failed at any commission.

How do we vet a seller before listing it?

A vendor must pass every one of these — the same checklist we give you for free in the Scam-Check:

  1. Realistic pricing consistent with factory list prices (no fake mega-discounts).
  2. Verifiable brand authorization from named factories (WM, Irontech, Zelex, Starpery…).
  3. Factory-photo policy: photos of your specific doll before dispatch, shipped only on your approval.
  4. Buyer-protected payment: PayPal and/or major credit cards.
  5. Organic review pattern, including resolved complaints — not a wall of invited 5-stars.
  6. Real contact surface: address, responsive support, coherent answers.
  7. Honest lead times for made-to-order dolls.
  8. Authentic imagery matching official factory catalogs.
  9. Written policies for shipping, customs, cancellation and defects.
  10. Established domain with operating history.

What will we never list?

We exclusively feature products depicting adults. We refuse to review, link to, or accept commissions on any product with a childlike appearance, we drop any vendor that stocks one, and we report them. This is non-negotiable and overrides every commercial consideration.

What can we not verify yet?

  • We're new, and our vetted list is short on purpose. We'd rather recommend one vendor we've actually checked than rank twelve we haven't.
  • Our one listed vendor does not have a clean record, and we publish it. Their public ratings diverge sharply between platforms, and the recurring complaints include listing photos that overstate the product and weak responsiveness after a complaint — which partly fails criterion 6 above. The evidence, the sources and our reasoning for keeping the listing anyway are on the picks page, not buried here.
  • Vendor vetting is point-in-time; vendors can degrade. If you have a recent experience — good or bad — with a vendor we list, email us. Buyer reports are how the list stays honest.
  • Product images on this site are placeholders until licensed imagery is in place. We will never present factory marketing photos as photos of delivered products.

Can you reuse our data?

Our data and tools are free to quote, embed or rewrite, with no attribution required and no affiliate links in any of the snippets we hand out. If you review dolls or answer buyer questions for other people, take what is useful.

How do you report an error on this site?

Factual error anywhere on this site? Tell us and we'll fix it and note the correction. That's the standard we hold vendors to; it applies to us first.
