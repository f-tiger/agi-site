Before you hire anyone to build AI,
find out if you need to.

Two things can end a problem: a tool that already does it, or a person who can. Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027. So before shopping for a person, it is worth asking whether this particular job needs one. This page triages your request against a published table: the tool that already does it, the recipe you can follow yourself, or an honest “yes, hire someone” — with what to ask for and what it should cost. Of the ten jobs currently in the table, six have an answer that is not a hire.

How this decides — and why it is not a chatbot

You could ask any assistant “who should build my AI agent” and get a fluent answer. The reason to use this page instead is that its answer is fixed, dated, and checkable. The routing table is a file you can read: every bucket names the tool it recommends, the price we observed, and where we saw it. When a claim here is wrong, it is wrong in public and can be corrected in one commit — a property no conversational answer has.

How good are these prices, honestly

Weaker than “sourced” makes them sound, so here is the grade rather than the adjective. As of 2026-08-30, 26 of the table’s 28 citations are third-party comparison articles rather than the vendor’s own pricing page — and several of those articles are published by companies selling a product that competes with the one they are used to price. Our build environment cannot reach vendor pricing pages, so first-party confirmation is still pending.

What that means for you: treat every figure here as a dated observation that tells you the right order of magnitude and the right question to ask — then check the number on the vendor’s own page before you pay anyone. It does not mean the figures are wrong. It means we have not personally watched the vendor say them, and a site whose whole claim is auditability should say which of its own claims are weakest.

The three verdicts

Solved nowA product that exists already does this. You are not hiring; you are signing up. We name it and what it costs.
Do it yourselfNo engineer required, but it needs an afternoon and a recipe. We give the recipe skeleton and the tools it uses.
Hire a humanThis genuinely needs a person. We say where to find them, what to put in the brief, and roughly what the work costs — so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

Matching runs in your browser by keyword overlap against the published table, so what you type never leaves this page unless you choose to submit it in the form below. To be exact rather than flattering: the site does record that a triage happened, which bucket it landed in, and how many characters you typed — never the text itself. You can verify both halves of that in the page source and the worker. If your need matches nothing confidently, the page says so rather than inventing a bucket.

If the table has no answer for you

The honest failure mode of a fixed table is a need it has never seen. Those are the ones worth collecting: an unmatched request is either a gap in the table or a genuinely novel piece of work. Post it and it gets read — and if it turns out an existing product solves it, that becomes a new public bucket everyone else gets for free.

Contact is optional and is used only to answer you. Nothing you write here is published, sold, or passed to a marketplace. Leave it blank and the request still counts — the text alone tells us what the table is missing.

How this site makes money, stated plainly

Right now, it does not. No link on this page is an affiliate link, and this site is not currently enrolled in any hiring platform’s affiliate programme. That will probably change — routing people to marketplaces is the intended business model — and when it does, this section changes with it, every paid link gets labelled on the link itself, and the change is dated here. (Status as of 2026-08-30.)

Referral revenue creates an obvious conflict: a site paid that way is tempted to tell everyone to hire. The standing rule against it is that “solved now” and “do it yourself” verdicts never carry a paid link. That count is computed from the published table on load and it counts paid links, not categories — so if a paid link ever appeared where the rule forbids one, this sentence would change by itself. The deploy pipeline also refuses to publish that combination at all.

What this site does not do

This matters more than the affiliate question, so it gets said plainly rather than in a footnote. This site does not vet anyone. It does not screen, background-check, interview, certify, rank or recommend individual freelancers or agencies, and it has no relationship with the people you will find on the platforms it links to. It is not an agency, not an employer, not a staffing firm, and not your representative. It does not guarantee that you will find someone, that work will be delivered, or what it will cost you.

The price ranges here are dated, sourced observations of what this kind of work has cost other people. They are not quotes, not estimates for your job, and not a promise about what you will be charged. The briefing checklist is what protects you in a hiring conversation — not this site, and not the platform.

Paying for work in stablecoin

Where a job does go to a person, the payment problem is real: cross-border fees, payout delays, and platform cuts are the most common complaint on the supply side. This site does not hold anyone's money — it never takes custody, runs no escrow, and is not a party to your transaction. What it can do is generate a direct, non-custodial payment link so a client and a builder can settle between their own wallets, and publish its own receiving address for the one thing it charges for.

Checking payment channel…

A note on scope, described as behaviour rather than as a legal label: displaying an address is not the same as holding funds for other people. This site does the former only. It never accepts money on behalf of a buyer or a seller, never releases funds, and never arbitrates a dispute. It also cannot refund, reverse or freeze any payment — including one made to it.

Stablecoin transfers are irreversible. If you send to a wrong address or on the wrong network, the funds cannot be recovered — not by this site, not by anyone. Confirm both the address and the network before you send.
