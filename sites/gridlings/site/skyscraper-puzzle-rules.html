Skyscraper Rules — Counting Towers You Can See

Every edge clue is a line of sight. Fill the grid with tower heights 1–5, one of each per row and column. An observer at each edge clue looks down that line: a tower is visible only if it is taller than everything before it, so the clue equals the number of visible towers. A clue of 1 means the tallest tower stands right at that edge; a clue of 5 means the heights ascend all the way (1-2-3-4-5).

1s and ns first

Clue 1 ⇒ the tallest tower (5) is adjacent to that clue. Clue 5 ⇒ the whole line ascends 1-2-3-4-5. Fill every forced cell these produce before anything else.

Opposite-edge sum

For the same line seen from both ends, clue_a + clue_b ≤ n + 1. Pairs that hit the bound (like 2 and 4 on a 5×5) sharply limit where the 5 can stand.

Ban the tall early

A clue of k means the first k−1 cells cannot hold the tallest tower — cross 5 out of them immediately, then re-apply the row/column no-repeat rule.

FAQ

What are the rules of a skyscraper puzzle?

Fill the grid with tower heights (1–5 on a 5×5) so every row and column contains each height once. Each clue on the edge counts how many towers are visible looking down that row or column from the clue's position — taller towers hide shorter ones behind them.

What does a 1 clue mean in skyscraper puzzles?

A 1 means only one tower is visible from that edge, which forces the tallest tower (5 on a 5×5) to stand immediately next to that clue.

What is the opposite-clue trick in skyscraper puzzles?

For any line, the two clues at its opposite ends satisfy a + b ≤ n + 1, where n is the grid size. Clue pairs near that bound pin down the position of the tallest tower quickly.
