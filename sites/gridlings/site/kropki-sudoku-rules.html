Kropki Rules — Black Dots, White Dots, and the Missing Dot

Kropki has exactly three signals. A white dot between two cells means they differ by exactly 1 (like 3·4). A black dot means one is double the other (like 2·4). And no dot means neither relation holds — the missing dot is a real constraint, not an absence of information. A 1-2 pair satisfies both relations; by convention it is marked with a black dot. Rows and columns still contain each number once, like any Latin square.

The rules

RuleWhat it means
Latin squareEvery row and every column contains each number exactly once.
White dotThe two cells differ by exactly 1 — consecutive numbers in either order.
Black dotOne cell is exactly double the other (1·2, 2·4 on a 5×5).
No dotNeither relation holds — this negative constraint eliminates as many candidates as the dots do.
1-2 convention1 and 2 both differ by one and double — the pair is marked black by convention.

Start from the black dots

On a 5×5 only 1·2 and 2·4 can sit under a black dot, so every black dot forces a 2 into one of its two cells. A chain of two black dots is even stronger: it must be 1-2-4.

Use the silence

A cell next to a 3 with no dot between them cannot be 2, 4, or 6. On boards with few or zero givens — most dailies here start empty — the dotless edges do most of the work.

Parity walks

White-dot chains alternate odd/even. Trace a chain from any placed number and half the candidates disappear along the whole chain.

FAQ

What do the dots mean in kropki sudoku?

A white dot between two cells means the numbers differ by exactly 1. A black dot means one number is exactly double the other. If there is no dot, neither relation holds — that negative constraint is a full part of the rules.

What about 1 and 2 in kropki — black or white dot?

1 and 2 satisfy both relations (they differ by 1, and 2 is double 1). The standard convention, used here, is to mark a 1-2 pair with a black dot.

Can a kropki puzzle have no given numbers at all?

Yes. The dot and no-dot pattern can force a unique solution on its own — most daily boards on this site start completely empty and are machine-verified to be solvable by pure deduction.
