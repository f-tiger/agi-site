The 100 %/yr target, written as arithmetic

A reader asked this ledger to aim at doubling every year. That target has an exact answer, and it is not a matter of choosing a bolder strategy. Hold anything at leverage L and your compound growth rate is L·μ − L²σ²/2, where μ is the excess return and σ the volatility. That expression is a downward parabola: it peaks at the Kelly leverage and falls again above it, so there is a ceiling no amount of borrowing can pass. The ceiling is exp(S²/2) − 1, and it depends on one number only — the Sharpe ratio S = μ/σ.

Leverage column assumes the strategy's own volatility is 20% a year; the ceiling column does not depend on volatility at all. Growth rates are continuous and converted once at the end, and the arithmetic ignores borrowing costs, fees and taxes, all of which push the requirement higher rather than lower.

So the target is a Sharpe problem, not a leverage problem. Doubling every year needs a sustained Sharpe of at least 1.177, and roughly 1.36 if you size at half Kelly, which is what most people who have lived through a drawdown actually do. A broad equity index has historically run closer to the top of the first four rows than the bottom four. Model scoreboard, in the literature's units

Return-prediction papers report two things: monthly out-of-sample R² and rank information coefficient. Both models here are graded in exactly those units, so the comparison to published work is a comparison rather than a flourish. A month is scored only once the month after it has finished, and months before the models started are never scored — there is no backtest on this page, because a curve computed today would be the one number here nobody could check against a public execution date.

FAQ

Can this ledger reach 100% a year?

Only if one of its arms sustains a Sharpe ratio above about 1.18. Compound growth at leverage L is L·μ − L²σ²/2, which peaks at the Kelly leverage and falls again above it, so the highest annual return any leverage can reach is exp(S²/2) − 1, where S is the Sharpe ratio. Doubling every year therefore needs S of at least 1.177, or about 1.36 if you size at half Kelly. At a Sharpe of 1.0 the ceiling is 64.9%, and at 0.6 it is 19.7%. No leverage setting substitutes for the Sharpe ratio, so this ledger publishes each arm's measured figure instead of a target.

Is the AI trading ledger investment advice or a signal service?

No. It is a paper-trading experiment with rules fixed before the start date. No security is recommended, nothing is executed for anyone, no real money is involved, and positions are shown only after the paper execution date. Nothing on this page is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Why publish a paper ledger at all?

Because /do-ai-trading-agents-work holds vendors to a standard — positions timestamped before outcomes, every result graded, misses never deleted, methodology fixed in advance — and the only honest way to demand that standard is to meet it ourselves. The ledger is the demonstration, not a product.

What happens if one arm beats the S&P 500?

Nothing automatic. The pre-registered read date is 2027-03-08 (six months). On that date every arm's return, maximum drawdown and excess versus SPY are published as they are. Six months of paper results in one market regime is not evidence of skill, and the page will say so.
