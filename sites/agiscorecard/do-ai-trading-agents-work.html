FAQ

Do AI trading agents actually make money for their users?

There is no audited public evidence that they do. As of August 2026 no retail AI-trading-agent vendor publishes an independently verifiable live track record of user returns. What is documented instead: active retail traders overwhelmingly lose (97% of Brazilian futures day traders active 300+ days lost money; fewer than 1% of Taiwan day traders were persistently profitable), and backtests — the industry's main sales exhibit — are systematically inflatable.

Why are backtests not proof that a trading strategy works?

Because trying enough strategy variants on the same history will always produce a spectacular in-sample result by chance — Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu formalized this as backtest overfitting (Notices of the AMS, 2014). The pattern shows up in live data too: McLean and Pontiff (Journal of Finance, 2016) found published strategy returns decay roughly 26% out-of-sample and 58% post-publication.

Why is so much money being invested in AI trading agents in 2026?

The funding wave is real: Chinese press (Sina Tech, PEdaily, 36Kr) reported on August 17, 2026 that PandaAI closed three rounds in two months, and agent-sector startups reportedly raised about $633M across 12 days in early August. Funding proves investor appetite for the picks-and-shovels business — selling tools to would-be traders — not that the tools' users make money. Those are different businesses.

What evidence would change this verdict?

A vendor publishing a falsifiable live ledger: positions or signals timestamped before outcomes, all results graded including losses, misses never deleted, methodology fixed in advance — the shape of record we maintain ourselves in the SunWatch track record. If an AI trading agent vendor publishes that and it shows net user-level outperformance after costs, this page's verdict flips. None has, as of August 2026.
