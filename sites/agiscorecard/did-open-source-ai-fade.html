Did open-source AI fade, as predicted?

Evidence reviewed November 25, 2026 · Ledger dated September 6, 2026

What the November review found

The September 6 ledger verdict remains Wrong. Leopold Aschenbrenner's 2024 prediction rested on the idea that proprietary models would preserve a durable advantage. Epoch AI's historical comparison put the best open-weight models an average of about four months behind the closed frontier from January 1 through May 28, 2026. This rises to about six months if the open model's ECI point estimate must exceed the earlier closed model's. Epoch AI's original study and data

During this review, which covered October 27 through November 25, 2026 (UTC), we checked Epoch AI's November 4 switch to a Bayesian ECI model and a November analysis of gaps on individual benchmarks. The later sources use different methods and do not give a like-for-like replacement for the historical average. Epoch AI methodology update · November benchmark comparison and methods

When we would reopen this verdict. It returns to Open if the open-weight gap widens past roughly 18 months for two consecutive frontier generations. The sources reviewed do not establish that this condition was met.

A capability lead is not a permanent moat

Epoch measured an average open-weight capability lag of about four months from January 1 through May 28, 2026, or about six months under a stricter comparison. Its November methodology update did not re-estimate that gap. The ledger verdict remains Wrong.

The prediction vs reality

In Situational Awareness, the geopolitical argument rests on a specific premise: model weights and algorithmic secrets are the crown jewels, and whoever locks them down converts compute concentration into durable national advantage. That is the foundation of the essay's "Lock Down the Labs" program.

In the period Epoch AI studied, capable open-weight models approached earlier frontier levels within months on the general ECI. Epoch AI separately examined technical advances such as Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts. Epoch AI's open models report

Why this is the clearest miss

If intelligence is cheap and diffuse, compute concentration buys less geopolitical advantage than the essay's model assumes — and the arms-race logic that motivates a national "Project" weakens. Because roughly a third of the essay's policy conclusions depend on the moat holding, this miss has outsized implications.

The strongest counterargument

The narrow reading of the claim is that the very best models remain proprietary. The original study found a closed-model lead during its observation window. The broader reading asks whether that advantage lasts long enough to support the essay's policy conclusions. We grade the broader claim and publish the rule for reopening it. The earlier two-year retrospective separately discusses what would happen if open-weight performance were shown to depend on distillation and that route were durably blocked.

A distinction that sharpens this debate: a capability lead (frontier labs ship first — still true, and consistent with capex pouring into proprietary labs) is not a diffusion moat (the lead staying scarce). The essay's geopolitical argument needs the second — locking down weights was supposed to deny adversaries the capability, not just delay them. If a near-frontier open model is downloadable months later, the lockdown buys time, not denial. Investors betting on the first proposition and this verdict grading the second can both be right at once.

Frequently asked questions

Did open-source AI fade as Aschenbrenner predicted?

No, in the September 6, 2026 ledger. From January 1 through May 28, 2026, Epoch AI estimated a general ECI lag of about four months for the best open-weight models, or six months under a stricter comparison. The November 25 review retained the verdict.

Are DeepSeek and Qwen just distilling Western models?

Distillation allegations exist, but Epoch AI characterizes innovations like Multi-head Latent Attention and fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts as genuine advances. A world where the frontier can be cheaply distilled also undermines the durable-moat claim.

Why does the open-source verdict matter?

Roughly a third of Situational Awareness's policy program ('Lock Down the Labs') assumes weights and algorithms are a durable moat. If AI diffuses cheaply, that geopolitical logic weakens.
